Budget spreadsheet: what a good template needs (and a free way to start)

Updated 2026-10-29

Most budget spreadsheets are abandoned within a month. The ones that survive share six features.

The six must-haves

  1. A transactions log, not a monthly grid. Typing each expense in a list with a date, category and amount takes seconds; the sheet should sum them into categories by itself.
  2. Budget vs actual per category, with overspent categories highlighted.
  3. Drop-down categories so "Groceries" and "groceries" do not become two things.
  4. A year view to spot the months that always blow up (December, insurance renewals).
  5. Savings goals with a date, converted into a monthly amount.
  6. A dashboard: income, spending, left over, savings rate. If you have to hunt for the number, you stop looking.

Free 10-minute version

Make a sheet called Log with columns Date, Category, Description, Amount. On a second sheet list your categories with a monthly budget, and next to each use =SUMIFS(Log!D:D, Log!B:B, A2, Log!A:A, ">="&DATE(2026,10,1), Log!A:A, "<="&DATE(2026,10,31)) for the actual. Add a conditional-format rule that turns the row red when actual > budget. That is 80% of the value.
