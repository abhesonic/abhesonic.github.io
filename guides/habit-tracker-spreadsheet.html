Habit tracker spreadsheet: streaks, weekly goals and a year heat-map in Excel or Google Sheets

Updated 2026-11-05

Apps lock your history inside them. A spreadsheet habit tracker is yours, works on your phone through Google Sheets, and can show streaks and a heat-map of your whole year. Here is the layout and the formulas.

Layout: one row per day, one column per habit

Put dates in column A (one row per day for the whole year) and one habit per column from B onward. Mark a done day with x. Freeze the first row and column so the names stay visible on a phone. A conditional format with =$A2=TODAY() highlights today's row.

The formulas

  • Done today (all habits): =COUNTIF(INDEX(B:P,MATCH(TODAY(),A:A,0),0),"x")
  • Streak helper for a habit in column B, starting in row 2: =IF(B2="x",N(Z1)+1,0) in a helper column Z, filled down. Each cell counts the days in a row up to that date.
  • Current streak: the helper value on today's row, or yesterday's if today is not ticked yet: =MAX(INDEX(Z:Z,MATCH(TODAY(),A:A,0)),INDEX(Z:Z,MATCH(TODAY()-1,A:A,0)))
  • Longest streak: =MAX(Z:Z)
  • This week (week starting Monday): =COUNTIFS(A:A,">="&TODAY()-WEEKDAY(TODAY(),2)+1,A:A,"<="&TODAY(),B:B,"x"). Compare it with a weekly target such as "gym 4× a week" – weekly targets are kinder than perfect daily streaks.
  • Month score: ticks this month ÷ days passed this month, formatted as a percentage.

The year heat-map

Build a calendar grid (12 rows for months, 31 columns for days). In each cell, count whether the habit was done on that date, for example =COUNTIFS($A:$A,DATE(year,month,day),B:B,"x"), then add a colour-scale conditional format. One look shows the weeks you slipped and the months you were strongest.

Tips that make it stick

  1. Start with 3–5 habits. You can add more once ticking takes ten seconds.
  2. Track the smallest version of the habit ("read 1 page"), not the ideal one.
  3. Do not reset to zero emotionally after a missed day: the "never miss twice" rule beats a perfect streak.
