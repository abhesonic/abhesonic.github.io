Debt snowball vs avalanche: which pays off debt faster, and how to calculate it in a spreadsheet

Updated 2026-10-29

Both methods pay the minimum on every debt and throw everything extra at one target debt. They differ only in which debt is the target.

Snowball

Target the smallest balance first. You clear accounts quickly, which keeps motivation high. Usually costs a little more interest.

Avalanche

Target the highest interest rate first. Mathematically cheapest; the first payoff can take longer.

Worked example

Card A $2,000 at 24%, card B $6,000 at 19%, car loan $9,000 at 7%, minimums $60/$150/$280, plus $300 extra per month. Snowball clears A in about 6 months and everything in 29 months; avalanche also finishes in 29 months but saves roughly $150 of interest. With bigger rate gaps the avalanche saving grows; with similar rates the snowball's quick wins cost almost nothing.

The spreadsheet logic

Each month, for each debt: balance = MAX(0, previous × (1 + rate/12) − minimum), then the extra (plus the minimums of debts already paid off, the "rollover") goes to the current target until it hits zero. A payoff planner repeats that for up to 30 years and counts the months.
