52-week savings challenge spreadsheet: totals, the reverse version and how to actually finish

Updated 2026-11-05

Save $1 in week 1, $2 in week 2 and so on until $52 in week 52. It sounds small, but by the end of the year you have $1,378 – and a habit. Here is how to set it up in Excel or Google Sheets so you can see at a glance whether you are on track.

How much you end up with

The weekly amounts are 1 + 2 + … + 52, which adds up to 52 × 53 ÷ 2 = 1,378. Change the step and the total scales with it:

Weekly stepWeek 52 depositTotal after 52 weeks
$1$52$1,378
$5$260$6,890
$10$520$13,780

Three versions, and which one people finish

  • Classic (increasing): easy start, but the biggest deposits land in November and December, right when holiday spending peaks. This is where most people quit.
  • Reverse (decreasing): start with $52 in January, when motivation is highest, and finish with $1 in December. Same total, much easier last quarter.
  • Same amount: $26.50 every week also gives $1,378. Boring, but the easiest to automate with a standing transfer.

The spreadsheet in five columns

Put your step (for example 1, 5 or 10) in cell B1 and a start date in B2. Then build a 52-row table starting in row 5:

  • Week (A): 1 to 52.
  • Due date (B): =$B$2+(A5-1)*7
  • Amount (C): classic =A5*$B$1, reverse =(53-A5)*$B$1.
  • Saved? (D): a checkbox (Google Sheets: Insert → Checkbox) or an "x".
  • Running total (E): =SUM($C$5:C5)

Then three summary cells tell you everything:

  • Saved so far: =SUMIF(D5:D56,TRUE,C5:C56) (use "x" instead of TRUE if you type x).
  • Should have saved by today: =SUMIF(B5:B56,"<="&TODAY(),C5:C56)
  • Ahead or behind: the difference between the two. A conditional format that turns it red when negative is enough motivation for most people.

The 100 envelope challenge

Number 100 envelopes from 1 to 100, pull one at random and put that many dollars in it. All 100 envelopes add up to 100 × 101 ÷ 2 = $5,050. Doubling every envelope gives $10,100. In a spreadsheet a 10 × 10 grid of numbers with a "done" mark and a green conditional format works better than a long list, because you can see which envelopes are left.

How to actually finish

  1. Keep the money in a separate savings account so it does not get spent by accident.
  2. Set a recurring transfer for the average weekly amount and top up the difference on the weekly check-in.
  3. Missed a week? Do not restart. Add the missed amount to the next two weeks; the "behind" cell shows you exactly how much.
