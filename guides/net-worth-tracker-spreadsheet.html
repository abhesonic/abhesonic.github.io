Net worth tracker spreadsheet: what to include and how to update it in 10 minutes a month

Updated 2026-11-05

Net worth is the one number that shows whether your money is moving in the right direction: everything you own minus everything you owe. Updating it once a month in a spreadsheet takes about ten minutes and is more motivating than any budget.

What to include

AssetsDebts
Checking and savings accountsMortgage
Brokerage and investment accountsCar loans
Retirement accounts (401(k), IRA, pension value)Student loans
Home (a conservative estimate, minus ~6% selling costs)Credit card balances
Vehicles (what they would sell for today)Personal loans, buy-now-pay-later

Leave out furniture, electronics and clothes: they are hard to value and rarely sold.

The monthly snapshot layout

One row per account, with a column for its type (Asset or Debt) and a category, then one column per month. On the first of each month, type the current balances into the new column. Never overwrite old months: the history is the point.

The formulas

  • Total assets for the month in column D: =SUMIF($B:$B,"Asset",D:D)
  • Total debts: =SUMIF($B:$B,"Debt",D:D)
  • Net worth: assets − debts.
  • Change vs last month: this month's net worth − last month's; and since the start: this month − the first month.
  • Debt-to-asset ratio: debts ÷ assets. Watching it fall is often more encouraging than watching net worth rise.
  • Months to your goal: (goal − current net worth) ÷ average monthly growth. Average monthly growth = (latest − first) ÷ number of months.

Your FI number

A common rule of thumb for financial independence is yearly spending × 25 (the same as dividing by 4%, the "4% rule"). Spending $40,000 a year gives an FI number of $1,000,000. Treat it as a direction, not a guarantee: returns, inflation and taxes all move it.
